## Ranked candidates

### Rank 1 — Weighted BP Decoder Ensembles via Joint End-to-End Training (hb-276 draft)

**Technique:** arXiv:2610.33559, "Joint End-to-End Training of Weighted BP Decoder
Ensembles" by Felix Krieg, Paul Bezner, and Stephan ten Brink (IMS/University of
Stuttgart; same group as RBE arXiv:2608.09560). Submitted 2026-10-24 to cs.IT. 6-page
IEEE conference submission.

The core idea: all members of an ensemble of weighted BP (WBP) decoders share the same
parity-check matrix H and the same flooding BP schedule, but each member has its own
set of trainable real-valued edge weights on the Tanner graph. Members are trained
jointly, with a "best-member" loss that directly rewards the best-performing member at
each SNR point — not the ensemble average. This forces members to specialize to different
error regions, even at the cost of individual FER. The optimal training objective among
four compared variants is the best-member loss.

**Measured evidence:**
- PEG(64,32) LDPC, AWGN, E=10 members, 20 BP iterations per member:
  - +0.66 dB over a single (unweighted) BP decoder at FER 10⁻⁴
  - 0.23 dB less SNR required than the published specialized BP-RNN ensemble (Rosseel
    et al., hb-255) at FER 10⁻⁴
  - All trained individual members show *higher* standalone FER than the unweighted
    baseline — specialization comes at individual cost; ensemble gain is emergent
- CCSDS(128,64) LDPC: similar ensemble gains reported; precise dB figure not found in
  snippets, but the paper claims the mechanism generalizes across codes
- The gains are unverified independently; this is a 6-page conference preprint

**Primary source:**
- arXiv:2610.33559 abstract + HTML: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33559

**Applicability to pancetta:**
- FT8 uses LDPC(174,91), rate 0.52. PEG(64,32) and CCSDS(128,64) are the test codes;
  LDPC(174,91) is in the applicable block-length range but 2.7× longer than PEG(64,32).
  Gains likely smaller; must be measured on the actual code.
- Implementation path: weighted BP is standard BP with per-edge scalar multipliers on
  variable-to-check and check-to-variable messages. Pancetta's current SP-BP kernel
  (`pancetta-ft8/src/ldpc.rs`) would need per-edge weight tables and a training harness.
- Training: gradient-based over differentiable WBP (Adam, standard). Training corpus can
  be synthetic AWGN at the FT8 operating range (−20 to −12 dB SNR). The "best-member"
  loss differentiates through E independent WBP forward passes — feasible on GPU;
  CPU training on LDPC(174,91) is slow but possible.
- Once trained, inference is pure weighted BP (no additional structure vs. standard BP,
  just different constants). Wall-clock cost = E × (single WBP pass) — same as running
  E parallel BP decoders. At E=10 and 20 iterations, this is 2× the cost of current
  OSD-3 at full depth; at E=4 it may be cheaper.
- Relationship to hb-269 (RBE): RBE uses row-boosting (untrained, greedy selection per
  member). This paper uses end-to-end trained edge weights. The paper directly benchmarks
  against RBE and shows the trained approach wins. They are structurally compatible —
  one could initialize with RBE weights and fine-tune — but this paper supersedes RBE
  as the preferred exploration path if training infrastructure is available.
- Relationship to hb-255 (BP-RNN diversity): the paper beats the Rosseel BP-RNN ensemble
  by 0.23 dB at E=10 with far lower training complexity (plain WBP vs. RNN). Demotes
  hb-255 further.
- Priority relative to existing bank: lower than hb-271 AP-llrc (+0.36 dB confirmed from
  WSJT-X ablation; no training required) and hb-273 (gated OSD-3, +0.35 dB confirmed,
  short probe). Higher than hb-269 (RBE) since this paper directly outperforms RBE.
  The training requirement is the main blocker — GPU or long CPU training run needed.
- License: academic (arXiv preprint, no GPL code found; implementation is novel).
  Clean-room implementation path is open.

### Rank 2 — WSJT-X 3.3.0-beta5: FT8 "reduce repeated work" latency optimization

**Technique:** WSJT-X 3.3.0-beta5 (released 2026-10-28, five days after last scan's
cutoff) includes an FT8 decoder performance commit: "ft8: reduce repeated work and
decode latency" by David Christle. This is framed as a latency/efficiency fix rather
than a sensitivity improvement.

**Primary source:**
- WSJT-X GitHub releases, tag v3.3.0-beta5:
  https://github.com/WSJTX/wsjtx/releases/tag/v3.3.0-beta5
- mfsk-core (the Rust reimplementation) issue #587 independently identified the same
  class of problem: "Decoder<Ft8>: a second pass over a slot already decoded re-pays the
  whole search, and a budget cannot cut the fixed part" (jl1nie/mfsk-core#587).

**Measured evidence:** None (changelog entry only; no before/after decode-rate numbers).
The mfsk-core #587 framing suggests this is about multi-pass decode efficiency: a second
or later decode pass over a slot that was already decoded re-executes the full Costas
sync search (the expensive fixed part), even if nothing changed. Eliminating this could
reduce wall-clock per slot without affecting decode results.

**Applicability to pancetta:**
- pancetta-ft8's multi-pass architecture (`pancetta-ft8/src/decoder.rs`) runs multiple
  candidate-collection passes, each triggering a full sync search on the residual. If
  Christle's fix is about caching the sync search output across passes (reusing the
  candidate list), this would be a latency improvement (faster throughput, more passes in
  budget). This does NOT improve recall by itself.
- mfsk-core issue #587 is architecturally relevant: caching FFT/sync results between
  passes is an optimization pancetta may not fully exploit (check `fft_cache` use in
  `pancetta-ft8/src/decoder.rs` across pass boundaries).
- Decode-rate impact: minimal on correctness. Wall-clock impact: unknown without reading
  the commit diff; could be significant for multi-pass configurations.
- License: WSJT-X is GPL-2.0; a "spec first" firewall applies. The *optimization pattern*
  (cache sync results across passes) is architecturally obvious once stated; clean-room
  implementation is straightforward without reading source.

**Not bank-worthy as a decode-sensitivity candidate.** Noted as status update for hb-271
(WSJT-X 3.3.0 tracker); architecturally relevant to any future multi-pass timing
optimization probe.

### hb-269 (RBE) row-selection algorithm — more detail confirmed

From arXiv snippet access to 2608.09560: the ensemble construction is greedy — each
member is built from the individual candidate row set with the highest "rescue rate"
(fraction of BP failures rescued by boosting that row set) relative to plain BP. Boosting
a row is effective primarily when its support variable nodes are error-free; coverage
peaks at two boosted rows per member. The best single member is not necessarily part of
the best ensemble (ensemble complementarity matters). This details the §III-C–III-D
construction the Oct 26 scan needed for the probe design.

**Action for hb-269:** probe design can now proceed. The rescue-rate metric is
estimable from the existing pancetta-research BP-failure instrumentation
(`batch91_hb250_failed_candidates.rs` style). Greedy row selection: for each candidate
row r, measure fraction of BP failures in a design corpus rescued when row r's messages
are boosted by a scalar factor (1.5–3×); take the top-K rows per member; diversify across
members. K=2 per member is the paper's recommendation; ensemble size K=8 is a reasonable
first probe. **Remains de-prioritized below hb-273 and hb-271 per Oct 26 assessment**;
new hb-276 (trained WBP-E) should be explored in parallel or before hb-269 if GPU is
available, since it directly outperforms RBE.

## Noted but not bank-worthy

### Alpha-stable LDPC + ORBGRAND NSF paper

A NSF-hosted paper (via par.nsf.gov) reports ORBGRAND with alpha-stable LLRs achieving
+2–3 dB over Gaussian-assumption decoding for 5G NR CA-Polar and CRC codes. Not
applicable: GRAND/ORBGRAND is documented dead-end for FT8 (hb-260; LDPC(174,91) at rate
0.52 is far outside the tractable guessing regime). ORBGRAND's advantage over BP is
specifically in the LLR-computation step; pancetta's hb-256 (now SHIPPED-FT8-K6)
directly addresses the alpha-stable LLR problem via the knee function.

### ft8ts (TypeScript FT8 port) and go-ft8 — no new technique

ftts (e04/ft8ts) is a TypeScript port of WSJT-X v2.7.0 Fortran. go-ft8
(ColonelBlimp/go-ft8) is a Go FT8 codec. Both mirror WSJT-X and contain no new decoder
technique.
