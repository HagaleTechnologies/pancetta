## Noted but not bank-worthy

### arXiv:2509.25580 — Neural-Model-Augmented Hybrid NMS-OSD (Sep 29, 2025)

**Technique**: Serially couples a Normalized Min-Sum (NMS) decoder with OSD.
Two neural components are added: (1) a Decoding Information Aggregation (DIA)
model — a convolutional neural network trained on the per-bit soft-output
*trajectory* of the NMS decoder across its iterations, used to refine bit
reliability estimates for OSD; (2) a sliding-window-assisted (SWA) model that
enables early termination of the test-error-pattern (TEP) traversal. Tested on
LDPC, BCH, and RS codes.

Authors: Guangwen Li and Xiao Yu. Preprint Sep 2025, journal version published
Springer EURASIP J. Wireless Commun. Netw. 2026 (DOI: 10.1186/s13638-026-02654-1).
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25580

**Why not bank-worthy**:
1. **NMS-specific DIA**: The DIA model is trained on NMS trajectory data.
   Pancetta uses sum-product BP (SP-BP), not NMS; the DIA features (iteration
   extrinsics from NMS) are not directly transferable. Retraining on SP-BP
   trajectories is architecturally identical to **hb-064** (DIA-augmented OSD
   with iteration-trajectory features, arXiv:2404.14165), which was the parent
   hypothesis of PAN-9 and is already in the bank: hb-064 ran three sessions
   plus PAN-9's documented-null A/B on Sep 1, 2026 (partial corpus — 5,907
   labels; a 10^5–10^6-label follow-up is recommended before DIA is fully
   closed, per the PAN-9 verdict).
2. **SWA stopping is distinct from PAN-9 scope**: The sliding-window stopping
   classifier (SWA) is a *learned* model that predicts whether continued TEP
   traversal is expected to improve the current best estimate. PAN-9 tested
   neural *bit-ordering* (DIA); Pancetta's current OSD uses deterministic
   stopping; a CRC-pass collects the best candidate per order (not a stop —
   deeper orders still run unless an exit fires). The three deterministic exits
   are: (a) `accept_immediately_below` soft-distance threshold for immediate
   within-order early exit (`osd.rs:887-890`); (b) `DecodeBudget::has_time()`
   checkpoints that skip an entire order if budget is exhausted (`osd.rs:860`
   and equivalents at orders 2/3, `osd.rs:911,957`); (c) `passes_acceptance_gate`
   after each order completes — if the best CRC-valid candidate satisfies both
   `max_soft_distance` and `max_hard_errors`, deeper orders are skipped
   (`osd.rs:895-903, 944-948`). A learned SWA would need to be integrated and
   measured against all three of these order-level exits. SWA has not been
   probed. However, with depth-0/1 (0–91 TEPs), the wall-clock savings from
   stopping early are negligible. The main depth-2+ concern is cost control.
3. **PAN-9 verdict**: The Sep 1, 2026, A/B on depth-1 neural OSD (corpus: 5,907
   labels, 60 training epochs) showed D vs A: real recall gain (+5/+30 on
   hard-200/1000) but hard gate failure (noise FP 3→4, +1, disqualifying); D vs C
   (shipped-weights control): not significant. The DIA+OSD path is not abandoned
   — it is waiting on a larger corpus and a noise-FP-safe configuration — but
   the DIA ordering component of this paper provides no new lever to unlock it;
   the SWA stopping component is a separate potential lever (see source verdict).

Source: arXiv:2509.25580. Not bank-worthy for the DIA ordering component (maps to
hb-064 and hb-258, already in the bank). The SWA learned stopping classifier maps to
hb-064 (`evidence_for` entry, `hypothesis_bank.md:1080`) and is documented in
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-04-18-neural-osd-design.md:259-269` (explicit SWA
section, deferred from the current implementation). SWA remains untested by PAN-9.
Two revisit conditions: (1) **depth-1 experimental arm for FP control** — production
default is depth-0 (no TEP traversal); a stopping classifier at depth-1 could reduce
FP exposure seen in PAN-9 (B/C/D all raised noise FP vs. depth-0 arm A); (2) **depth-2+
cost control** — at depth-2 (4,095 TEPs), early stopping becomes the primary cost lever.

### arXiv:2607.14589 — Quasi-BP and Neural-Network Check Node Processing for BCH Codes (Jul 16, 2026) *(carry-forward from Jul 20 scan)*

**Technique**: Quasi-BP decoding of BCH codes exploiting code automorphisms
and an optimized redundant parity-check matrix; a neural-network variant
replaces check-node tanh/atanh computations with a lightweight CNN trained
under a triple-constraint loss. The CNN variant achieves near-identical FER
(within ~0.25 dB gap) while eliminating transcendental function calls.

Author: Guangwen Li (Shandong Technology and Business University). URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14589

**Why not bank-worthy**:
- **BCH-specific**: The quasi-BP technique exploits BCH code automorphisms —
  cyclic group structure that LDPC(174,91) does not share. The redundant
  parity-check matrix construction is likewise BCH-specific. The approach does
  not transfer to pancetta's LDPC(174,91) decoder.
- The CNN check-node replacement idea is independently interesting for LDPC, but
  is already in the bank as hb-195 (factor-graph neural decoder with learned BP
  message functions replacing check-node updates). The BCH
  paper does not demonstrate the technique on LDPC or provide comparative FER
  against optimized LDPC BP — the claimed 0.25 dB gap is vs. LDPC of similar
  blocklength, not vs. the LDPC(174,91) FT8 code itself.

Source: arXiv:2607.14589. Not bank-worthy (BCH-specific; closest entry for the CNN check-node replacement component is hb-195, which covers learned message functions replacing BP updates in an LDPC factor graph).

### arXiv:2608.14280 — LLM-Assisted LDPC Decoding via Syndrome-Verified Semantic Priors (Aug 14, 2026) *(carry-forward from Aug 24 scan)*

**Technique**: When BP fails on an LDPC-coded natural-language transmission,
an LLM infers the intended characters from surrounding semantic context, and its
suggested corrections are verified against the LDPC parity-check syndrome before
being injected as soft LLR updates (syndrome-verified semantic priors). The
combined BP+LLM decoder outperforms plain BP on NL-payload transmissions.

Authors: Park, Lyu, Choi, Yang (POSTECH and Seoul National University). URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14280

**Why not bank-worthy**:
- **NL payload required**: The semantic prior mechanism exploits the LLM's
  probabilistic knowledge of natural-language character sequences — the LLM
  "guesses" an error correction from linguistic context. FT8's 77-bit messages
  are structured, machine-formatted fields (callsigns, Maidenhead grid, signal
  report), not natural language. A general-purpose NL LLM cannot infer the
  correct callsign from linguistic context — the mismatch is between the NL
  prior and FT8's code domain, not the structured-message prior concept itself.
  Note: the bank does contain FT8-structured message priors (hb-188
  callsign/grid/report grammar model, hb-191 cross-slot QSO state LM) that
  exploit FT8's structure; those are viable. This paper's NL LLM mechanism is
  not transferable, but structured-message analogues exist.
- The syndrome-verification step in this paper applies LDPC parity-check
  constraints to screen LLM-generated corrections *before* LLR injection —
  a pre-decode filter. This is distinct from FT8's CRC-14 outer code, which
  is a post-decode payload validity gate applied after a candidate is decoded,
  not a mid-traverse syndrome screener for proposed corrections.

Source: arXiv:2608.14280. Not bank-worthy (NL payload required; not applicable
to FT8's structured message format).

### arXiv:2603.20385 — Cost-Aware Neural Early Stopping for LC-OSD (Mar 20, 2026)

**Technique**: A neural early stopping (NES) protocol for Local Constraint OSD
(LC-OSD) that uses an FER-aligned training objective and a single tradeoff
parameter β (FER risk vs. TEP cost). At each checkpoint along the fixed TEP
traversal path, a lightweight model predicts whether continued enumeration is
expected to improve the current best codeword; if not, it terminates. Reported
result: significant average TEP count reduction (paper's primary claim) with
only marginal FER degradation. Simulated on LDPC codes including CCSDS (128,64).

Authors: Talha Akyildiz and Hessam Mahdavifar (University of Michigan, Ann Arbor).
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20385

**Why not bank-worthy as a standalone entry (production is depth-0)**:
1. **Depth-0/1 TEP counts are tiny**: Pancetta's production OSD is depth-0 (zero
   TEPs; only the reliability-ordered codeword is evaluated). Depth-1 OSD adds at
   most 91 TEPs (one bit flip over the 91 information bit positions). Early
   stopping on 91 TEPs provides minimal wall-clock savings: upstream Costas/FFT
   cost dominates overall elapsed time (not BP iteration count), and direct
   measurement (PAN-9, 2026-08-08) shows depth-0 and depth-1 elapsed times are
   essentially identical (1003.6 s vs 1000.3 s on the same corpus). Only at
   depth-2+ does TEP enumeration become the dominant cost.
2. **LC-OSD specific**: The "local constraint" (LC) component requires a local
   redundancy matrix derived from the parity-check matrix, used to apply
   syndrome constraints *during* TEP enumeration — pruning or scoring patterns
   before full candidate generation. Pancetta's OSD validates each TEP candidate
   after generation (CRC-14 via `try_solution`; LDPC validity enforced by
   construction from the reduced generator matrix) but does not prune patterns
   pre-generation using parity-check-matrix local constraints. Implementing
   LC-OSD would require restructuring the OSD stage around block-local
   redundancy matrices to gate or score patterns during enumeration.
3. **NES untested, not ruled out by PAN-9**: The NES stopping classifier
   predicts whether continued traversal will improve the best candidate — a
   different mechanism from DIA ordering. PAN-9's null (Sep 1, 2026) applies
   specifically to bit-ordering (DIA); it did not test stopping. The noise-FP
   regression in PAN-9 was attributed to enabling depth-1 itself across all
   arms (B/C/D all raised FP vs. A), so a stopping policy that terminates
   traversal earlier could even reduce that exposure. NES at depth-0/1 is not
   ruled out by PAN-9 — it is simply untested. Two distinct revisit conditions
   exist: (1) **depth-1 FP control**: PAN-9's depth-1 arm raised noise FP
   (3→4), attributed to enabling depth-1 itself across all arms; a learned
   stopping classifier at depth-1 could terminate traversal early enough to
   reduce that FP exposure — worth evaluating under hb-064 in a depth-1 experimental arm (production default is depth-0, no TEP traversal);
   (2) **cost control at depth-2+**: at depth-2 (4,095 TEPs), NES
   becomes the primary cost-control mechanism. Flag both under hb-064.

**Revisit conditions**: Two conditions apply. (1) **Depth-1 experimental arm for FP
control**: PAN-9's depth-1 arm raised noise FP (3→4 vs. depth-0 arm A), attributed to
enabling depth-1 itself; a learned stopping classifier at depth-1 that terminates
traversal early could reduce that FP exposure. Worth evaluating under hb-064 even
before depth-2 is reconsidered. (2) **Depth-2+ cost control**: at depth-2
(C(91,2) = 4,095 TEPs), NES becomes the primary cost-control mechanism. NES belongs
to the same hypothesis class as the SWA stopping classifier already captured under
hb-064 (`hypothesis_bank.md:1079-1081`) and deferred in
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-04-18-neural-osd-design.md:259-269`. NES supplies a
different objective (FER-aligned training loss) and a single β tradeoff parameter
rather than a running-score window, but it is a refinement of the same
stopping-classifier hypothesis rather than a new bank entry. Record
arXiv:2603.20385 as additional evidence for hb-064 under both conditions.

Source: arXiv:2603.20385. Not in prior scans. Not bank-worthy as a standalone
entry; NES maps to hb-064 (same stopping-classifier hypothesis class; different
objective and β tradeoff from SWA). **Revisit as evidence for hb-064 under both
conditions: (1) depth-1 experimental arm for FP control; (2) depth-2+ cost control.**
