## Ranked candidates

### 1. hb-271 — WSJT-X mainline v3.2.0-rc1 ft8var audit: QSO-state decoder + bmete/llre default metric  (priority 0.55)

**Technique — ft8var multithreaded decoder**: WSJT-X 3.2.0-rc1 introduces a second
FT8 decoder in `lib/ft8var/` (~55 Fortran files, `ft8bvar.f90` alone = 2976 lines vs.
`ft8b.f90`'s 533 lines in the standard decoder). The ft8var decoder is reached only
when the operator enables "Use multithreaded FT8 decoder" and requires QSO state
(last TX, last RX, nQSOProgress) plus a callsign database (ALLCALL7.TXT, ~10,000
entries) not needed by the standard decoder.

Key sub-techniques inside ft8var:

1. **QSO-state-dependent thresholds and AP levels**: sync and AP thresholds are
   adjusted per pass based on MyCall/HisCall and nQSOProgress. A DX call detected
   near the QSO frequency triggers OSD at depth 4 (vs. production depth 0; vs.
   WSJT-X standard depth 2–3 via ndepth).

2. **Hint decoders (FT8S, FT8SD variants)**: match received tones against a
   pre-computed set of QSO-specific expected messages WITHOUT running LDPC/OSD FEC.
   These provide a fast pre-filter pass; if a hint decoder fires, LDPC can be skipped
   or run at reduced cost. The "chkfalse8" component applies a false-decode filter
   analogous to hb-052 but in this QSO-state-aware context.

3. **Frequency-split parallelism**: the band is divided into ranges, one per
   OpenMP thread, with coordinated residual subtraction across workers.

4. **Variable pass counts**: operator-selectable 3, 6, or 9 passes (vs. fixed
   structure in the standard decoder) with per-pass sync thresholds 1.225/1.3/1.1
   (with low-threshold mode).

**Technique — bmete/llre default decoder metric**: The standard WSJT-X FT8 decoder
is also updated in v3.2.0-rc1 with a new 5th metric, `bmete`/`llre`: the per-bit
largest-magnitude value across the existing metrics a/b/c. This provides an
alternative LLR reliability ordering for OSD—instead of using a single metric's
magnitude, it takes the pointwise max across multiple independent LLR computations,
yielding a bit-reliability estimate that is at least as confident as the best single
metric on each bit.

**Primary source**: https://github.com/WSJTX/wsjtx, v3.2.0-rc1, released 2026-09-24.
jl1nie/mfsk-core issue #463 ("FT8: WSJT-X's multithreaded FT8 decoder (lib/ft8var,
'ft8md') — a second, QSO-state-dependent decoder; decide whether to port") confirmed
ft8var as new in v3.2.0-rc1.

**Measured evidence**: No published sensitivity benchmark yet for ft8var vs. the
standard decoder. Community reports on WSJT-X 3.2.0-rc1 sensitivity are not yet
available (release was 4 days ago). The OSD depth-4 upgrade alone is expected to
recover a subset of the weak QSO frames that depth-0 misses (prior bank work: hb-053
graduated OSD gate=6 + iters=100; depth is a separate dimension).

**Applicability to pancetta**:
- pancetta-qso already tracks QSO state (nQSOProgress equivalent) — the state-
  dependence mechanism is a natural fit.
- The hint decoder (tone-only pre-check before LDPC) is architecturally compatible
  with pancetta's symbol-extraction pipeline.
- The bmete/llre reliability metric applies to any decoder that exposes per-bit
  LLR magnitudes from multiple computation paths; pancetta's SP-BP LLR and AP
  context LLRs could serve as the "metrics a/b/c" inputs.
- FT8VAR's callsign-database requirement (ALLCALL7.TXT) maps roughly to pancetta-dx's
  existing callsign lookup infrastructure and the existing CQ callsign list.

**License note**: WSJT-X mainline is GPL v2 (or later). Clean-room spec firewall
applies (`docs/PROVENANCE.md:23-30`): one contributor may read the GPL source and
produce a prose-only specification; the implementer never opens the GPL source.
The ft8var decoder components (FT8S, FT8SD, ft8bvar, chkfalse8) carry comments with
"ft8md w3sz" (W3SZ is the callsign of a known WSJT-X contributor), indicating a
contributed component inside the mainline GPL tree.

### 2. hb-272 — Dynamic Early Termination of Sequential Ensemble + OSD  (priority 0.28)

**Technique**: "When to Stop? Dynamic Early Termination of Sequential Ensembles"
(Bezner, Krieg, ten Brink; Universität Stuttgart; arXiv:2609.26215, Sep 2026) applies
dynamic ensemble termination (DET) to the sequential ensemble + OSD framework that
their group introduced in RBE (arXiv:2608.09560, hb-269) and SECS
(arXiv:2608.09563, hb-270). Members of the sequential ensemble are evaluated one at a
time; decoding stops when a decision-tree estimator determines that the expected gain
from activating remaining members falls below an offline-fitted risk threshold. This
removes OSD's inability to exploit the ensemble's natural syndrome-based stopping,
recovering most of the complexity reduction without sacrificing FER performance.

The framework instantiates with: layered normalized min-sum (NMS) component decoders,
a row-boosted ensemble structure (from RBE), conditional low-order OSD on the dual
code (from SECS), and a conventional low-cost decoder gate for the easy regime.

**Primary source**: arXiv:2609.26215, Paul Bezner, Felix Krieg, Stephan ten Brink
(Institut für Nachrichtentechnik, Universität Stuttgart). URL:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26215

**Measured evidence** (from search snippet; full paper proxy-blocked):
- Validated on IEEE 802.11 LDPC codes, blocklengths 648, 1296, and 1944 at rates 1/2
  and 5/6. These are much larger than FT8's LDPC(174,91).
- Paper is 6 pages (conference submission format); exact FER/BER numbers not obtained
  from snippet.

**Applicability**: Low at current state. DET reduces complexity of the RBE+OSD
combination, not sensitivity. The key unresolved prerequisite is whether RBE or SECS
achieves meaningful diversity on FT8's short, optimized irregular code
(SECS paper notes "optimized LDPC codes offer little graph diversity"; this is the
hb-269/hb-270 diversity-premise probe). The blocklength gap (648–1944 vs. 174 bits)
compounds the uncertainty. DET should be attempted only as a third step if the
diversity probe shows positive results for hb-269 or hb-270.

**License**: arXiv preprint (conference submission). No source code available.

## Noted but not bank-worthy

### arXiv:2608.04068, arXiv:2608.22685, arXiv:2609.29688 — GRAND family extensions

Three recent papers extend the GRAND/SOGRAND line:
- **arXiv:2608.04068** (Razeghi, Harvard; Aug 4): Tail-Calibrated SOGRAND for finite-memory
  noise-effect posteriors. ML-optimal GRAND variant for correlated/finite-memory channels.
- **arXiv:2608.22685**: Soft GRAND under channel switching and drift.
- **arXiv:2609.29688** (Razeghi, Harvard; Sep 2026): Soft GRAND under channel uncertainty —
  minimax posterior-envelope ordering and Gallager-exponent preservation.

All three fall within hb-260's documented dead-end: GRAND/ORBGRAND requires querying
all noise patterns of weight ≤ n_max for a length-n code; FT8's LDPC(174,91) at rate
0.52 with 83 redundancy bits makes query counts prohibitive at weak-signal SNRs. The
correlated-noise and channel-uncertainty extensions do not resolve the code-length
tractability problem. Not bank-worthy.
