## Noted but not bank-worthy

### arXiv:2607.14589 — Quasi-BP decoding for BCH codes (July 16, 2026)

**Technique**: A modified belief-propagation algorithm for BCH algebraic block
codes using a quasi-cyclic graph representation. Exploits BCH's algebraic
structure to improve BP convergence on the dense parity-check matrix of BCH
codes.

**Why not bank-worthy**: BCH codes are an entirely different code family from
FT8's LDPC(174,91). BCH codes have dense parity-check matrices derived from
polynomial algebra; FT8's LDPC code has a sparse parity-check matrix. The
decoding algorithms are structurally distinct — this technique specifically
exploits BCH's polynomial-ring structure, which does not exist in LDPC. The
result is not transferable. No bank entry.

### arXiv:2607.12187 — Auxiliary nodes for Quantum LDPC codes (July 13, 2026)

**Technique**: Introduces auxiliary variable nodes into Tanner graphs for
Quantum LDPC codes to resolve degeneracy in the quantum stabilizer formalism.
Improves BP convergence on quantum CSS codes.

**Why not bank-worthy**: Addresses quantum error correction, not classical
channel coding. Quantum LDPC codes have fundamentally different structure
(stabilizer formalism, non-binary GF(2)×GF(2) codes, degenerate errors) from
FT8's classical binary LDPC. The auxiliary-node construction is specific to
quantum degeneracy and has no classical analogue. Not applicable.

### arXiv:2607.13666 — Spectral methods for Quantum LDPC decoding (July 15, 2026)

**Technique**: Spectral graph theory applied to decoding quantum LDPC codes;
uses eigenvalue analysis of Tanner graphs to guide syndrome decoding.

**Why not bank-worthy**: Same quantum-only inapplicability as arXiv:2607.12187.
July 2026 arXiv shows a dense cluster of quantum LDPC papers that dominate the
cs.IT/eess.SP feed; all are orthogonal to FT8's classical binary LDPC.

### arXiv:2506.10513 — NN-Chase decoding for URLLC short codes (June 2026)

**Technique**: Neural network used to select the optimal test error patterns
(TEPs) for Chase-Pyndiah decoding of short block codes in URLLC scenarios.
Trains a small GRU network on per-candidate LLR vectors to predict which TEPs
are most likely to produce a codeword.

**Why not bank-worthy**: This is a neural enhancement of Chase/OSD-style
decoding. OSD already has ~0 recall contribution on pancetta's hard-200 corpus
(hb-014), so neural-improved OSD inherits the same recall ceiling. Additionally,
the GRU network requires offline training on FT8-specific channel distributions
(overlaps hb-255 BP-RNN+OSD territory and hb-258 trajectory-informed ordering)
— both of which remain pending probes requiring GPU infrastructure. The
NN-Chase paper does not offer a path that bypasses the hb-014 dead-end finding.
No distinct new entry; note here to preempt future resurface. If training
infrastructure (GPU) becomes available, this is subsumed by hb-255/hb-258.

### arXiv:2606.11401 — Max Coverage Chase decoding (June 2026)

**Technique**: Reformulates Chase decoding as a maximum-coverage combinatorial
optimization problem, selecting TEPs to maximise coverage of the information
bit space rather than selecting by LLR magnitude. Applied to hard-decision RS
and BCH codes.

**Why not bank-worthy**: Targets hard-decision decoders for RS and BCH codes.
FT8 uses soft-decision BP+OSD on an LDPC code. The "maximum coverage" metric
is derived from the algebraic structure of RS/BCH — there is no equivalent
structure in LDPC. Additionally, OSD dead-end (hb-014) applies: improvements
to the TEP selection step in a Chase-style decoder do not help if OSD
contributes ~0 TP gains on the hard-200 corpus.

### arXiv:2506.04839 — Chase-Pyndiah for product codes (June 2026)

**Technique**: Iterated Chase-Pyndiah decoding applied to product codes
(block × block). Each component decoder is a Chase-2 decoder; the product
structure enables iterative extrinsic exchange across rows and columns.

**Why not bank-worthy**: FT8 is not a product code; LDPC(174,91) has no row×
column structure to exploit. Chase-based approaches remain dead per hb-014.
