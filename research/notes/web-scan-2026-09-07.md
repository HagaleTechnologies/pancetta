## Ranked candidates

### 1. hb-270 — SECS: Shallow-Ensemble Extrinsic Combining → OSD  (priority 0.40)

**Technique**: SECS (Soft Ensemble-Combining Stage) runs M copies of a
belief-propagation decoder in parallel for only a few (shallow) iterations.
After early stopping, the extrinsic messages from all M members are combined
(soft-aggregated) to produce a reliability ordering of the systematic bits.
Because the members are shallow and diverse, they individually fail as
correctors but collectively produce a more reliable sorting of bit positions
than any single deeply-iterated decoder. A subsequent OSD re-encoding stage
uses this ensemble-derived ordering (instead of the single-decoder |LLR|
ordering) to construct the near-ML codeword.

The key observation that motivates the paper: "Shallow, diverse BP members
are poor correctors, but excellent sorters." The ensemble's soft-combined
extrinsics are more reliable than the final extrinsics of a single fully
converged decoder, because diverse members avoid sharing the same error-floor
trapping set.

**Why this is distinct from existing bank entries**:
- **hb-269 (RBE)**: RBE runs K fully converging members, accepts the first
  CRC-passing result. SECS does not converge fully and does not depend on
  any member individually passing CRC — it uses the combined extrinsic
  signal from M shallow members as an ordering input to OSD. Different
  mechanism (early-stopping ensemble → OSD vs. full-convergence ensemble
  → first-CRC-pass).
- **hb-257 (parallel ensemble + ML selection)**: Selects the best candidate
  from the ensemble. SECS does not select among ensemble outputs — it
  combines the extrinsic messages for reliability ordering and passes
  a single OSD stage.
- **hb-258 (trajectory-informed OSD ordering)**: Uses one decoder's per-bit
  LLR trajectories across iterations to order OSD. SECS uses the *combined*
  extrinsics of M diverse shallow decoders — a fundamentally different
  information source (ensemble diversity rather than single-decoder
  iteration history).
- **hb-255 (BP-RNN + OSD)**: Uses trained RNN decoders for diversity.
  SECS uses standard (unmodified or simply diverse) BP kernels with
  early stopping. Lower complexity per member.

**Primary source**: arXiv:2608.09563, Felix Krieg, Paul Bezner, Stephan
ten Brink (Universität Stuttgart). Submitted 2026-08-11 (one day after RBE,
arXiv:2608.09560). Same group; companion paper to RBE.
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09563

**Measured evidence** (from search results; arxiv.org proxy-blocked —
full paper not obtained; exact numbers may be from search snippet):
- BCH(63,30): SECS+OSD closes most of the gap to ML decoding with
  significantly fewer BP iterations than a single deep decoder.
- PG(2,8) overcomplete code: similar result.
- Search-designed (105,53) cyclic code: similar result.
- The paper explicitly notes: "Optimized LDPC codes offer little graph
  diversity: their only light dual codewords are the designed rows, so
  same-graph ensembles built by perturbation share most failure events
  and gain correspondingly little from combining." [from search excerpt]
- All three demonstration codes are non-LDPC or non-optimized. The paper
  does NOT demonstrate SECS on an optimized LDPC code.

**Applicability to pancetta**:
- Pancetta's decoder already has the two components SECS composes: layered
  BP (sum-product, 100 iterations) and OSD (depth 0, with optional depth 1
  behind `osd_depth`). The new component is the *soft extrinsic combining*
  stage between shallow-BP-ensemble and OSD.
- The paper's own diversity caveat is a strong negative signal for FT8's
  LDPC(174,91): FT8's parity-check matrix is designed/optimized (WSJT-X
  provenance), so it may have little graph diversity, and a same-graph
  SECS ensemble may share most failure events. This is the dominant
  uncertainty for this hypothesis.
- Architectural fit: a SECS wrapper would (a) run M instances of BP for
  a small number of iterations (e.g. 5-15 vs. the production 100), (b)
  soft-combine the extrinsic messages per bit, (c) pass the ordering to
  the existing OSD kernel. Steps (a) and (c) touch existing code; (b) is
  the new implementation task.
- Wall-clock: M × shallow-iters vs. 1 × 100 iterations. If M=4 and
  shallow-iters=10, that is 40 iteration-equivalents vs. 100 — faster
  than the current single-decoder path, but M must be large enough for
  diversity. If M=8 and shallow-iters=20, that is 160 equivalents (1.6×).
  The paper's "low-latency" claim rests on shallow iteration counts.
- FP safety: the OSD step still runs a CRC check, so FP risk is not
  structurally higher than the existing OSD-1 operating point (which
  already showed a noise-FP increase vs. OSD-0 per PAN-9; this would
  need separate measurement).

**Probe plan (premise-first)**:
1. **Premise probe**: measure the diversity of FT8's LDPC(174,91) graph
   before building SECS. Concretely: for a sample of hard-200 BP-failure
   cases, run M=4 shallow-BP (e.g. 10 iterations) instances with different
   random perturbations or LayeredBP orderings; compare the per-bit
   extrinsic variance across members vs. within a single 100-iter run.
   If per-bit variance across members is low (members agree on the same
   wrong bit ordering), SECS gain is expected to be near zero — SHELVE
   the hypothesis without further implementation. If variance is
   meaningful, proceed to step 2.
2. **Implement extrinsic combining**: add a soft-mean (or soft-max)
   combiner over M members' extrinsic message arrays at a specified
   early-stopping iteration.
3. **Sweep**: M ∈ {4, 8, 16}, early-stop ∈ {5, 10, 20} iterations; pass
   the combined reliability ordering to the existing OSD kernel at depth 0
   and depth 1. Measure on hard-200 + noise_1000 (same FP gate as PAN-9).
4. **Compare**: SECS ordering (combined extrinsics) vs. single-decoder
   |LLR| ordering (current default) and vs. trajectory-informed ordering
   (hb-258) at equal OSD depth — attribute gains specifically to the
   ensemble ordering vs. the OSD depth.

**License**: Academic (University of Stuttgart). No GPL firewall needed.

**Interaction with hb-269 (RBE)**:
The SECS paper's diversity finding directly informs hb-269's probe plan.
If the diversity premise probe (step 1 above) shows FT8's LDPC code has
low graph diversity, that is also a negative signal for RBE: RBE's row-
boosting mechanism (which creates diversity within the same H) would
similarly share failure events across members. A shared diversity probe
(step 1) is thus the gating prerequisite for BOTH hb-270 (SECS) and
hb-269 (RBE). Recommend running the diversity premise probe before
committing effort to either hb-269 or hb-270 implementation.

### Kong et al. Entropy 2026 (DOI 10.3390/e27090899) — hb-267 source paper now peer-reviewed

The preprint "An Efficient Two-Stage Decoding Scheme for LDPC-CRC
Concatenated Codes" (Kong, Liu, Shi, Miao; TechRxiv February 2026) that
spawned hb-267 (Two-Stage BP+GRAND-on-CRC) has been published in the
journal Entropy (MDPI), September 2026, DOI 10.3390/e27090899.

**What it confirms**: The two-stage BP+GRAND-on-CRC mechanism (BP first;
if LDPC CRC fails, run GRAND on the CRC portion to find an information
sequence satisfying the CRC check, re-encode to LDPC, select most likely)
is now peer-reviewed literature. No new algorithmic content beyond the
preprint. The abstract confirms the performance claim: "outperform the
traditional BP-based iterative algorithm with a large maximum number of
iterations, with relatively low average complexity."

**Status update for hb-267**: the paper basis is now peer-reviewed.
hb-267 remains PENDING (no experiment journals found; probe not started).
The journal DOI strengthens the evidentiary basis. Not a new bank entry.

### arXiv:2609.03256 — Iterative Semantic Decoding for Short Block Codes (September 3, 2026)

**Technique**: Iterative alternation between a channel decoder and a
language model. Transmitter applies a character-level interleaver and
encodes text segments into short block codes; receiver iterates: channel
decoder (BP or OSD) → deinterleaver → language model denoising →
verified characters fed back as a priori information for next iteration.
Claims ~1.5 dB BLER gain on SNLI corpus over AWGN.

**Why not bank-worthy**: Identical disqualifier to arXiv:2608.14280
(LLM-LDPC, Aug 2026 scan): requires a **natural-language** payload for
the language model's semantic prior to have any value. FT8 messages are
highly structured (callsign + callsign + grid/report/RR73/etc., 77 bits
total) and are not natural language — the language model cannot exploit
semantic structure in "W1AW/W8 N9TZK FN43." Not bank-worthy; noted to
prevent future resurface of the natural-language LM class.

ensemble decoding reference is a citation, not a new decoder algorithm.
FT8's LDPC(174,91) is not an MDS code. Not applicable.

### arXiv:2608.14479 — Sensing-Aided ORBGRAND (August 14, 2026)

**Technique**: Uses geometry-based environmental sensing (angle-of-departure/
arrival priors, not just pilot observations) to improve the LLR estimates
fed to ORBGRAND, yielding better reliability ordering and lower query count.
Simulation results on narrowband fading channels.

**Why not bank-worthy**: Two barriers:
1. **GRAND dead-ended for FT8's LDPC** (hb-260): GRAND and ORBGRAND target
   short high-rate codes; FT8's LDPC(174,91) at rate 0.52 with 83 parity
   bits is outside the tractable guessing regime.
2. **Geometry sensing not available**: The paper's "sensing" is angle-of-
   departure/arrival from a separate sensing subsystem. Pancetta operates
   on a single omnidirectional HF antenna with no direction-finding
   capability. Costas-array-based channel estimation (already in-tree) is
   conceptually related but only uses pilot symbols, not geometric priors.
   The technique's gain specifically comes from the geometric prior, not
   just from better Costas-based LLRs.
   
Noted here for completeness; the sensing-ORBGRAND direction may be
re-relevant if pancetta ever adds direction-finding or multi-antenna Rx,
but it is not applicable to the current single-antenna architecture. Not
bank-worthy; noted to prevent future resurface.

### arXiv:2608.22685 — Soft GRAND under Channel Switching and Drift (August 24, 2026)

**Technique**: Theoretical analysis of how Soft GRAND performs when the
posterior used for query ordering mismatches the true channel (due to
switching or drift). Derives log query rank bounds; shows that for
capacity-achieving uniform input, a state-path mixture achieves vanishing
error below the minimum constituent capacity. Also provides pilot-refresh
bounds for channel drift.

**Why not bank-worthy**: Theoretical analysis paper; no new decoding
algorithm. The findings are relevant to hb-267 (BP+GRAND-on-CRC) in the
sense that HF channel drift affects how well GRAND's LLR ordering matches
the true CRC-layer error pattern, but this paper does not introduce a new
probe-worthy mechanism — it provides the theoretical basis for why
pilot-refresh (e.g. Costas sync) bounds the mismatch. Similar in nature
to arXiv:2603.07526 (ORBGRAND finite-BL analysis, treated as "supports
hb-267 but not standalone entry" in Aug 3 scan). Not bank-worthy.

### arXiv:2606.28030 — RS-ORBGRAND (June 26, 2026)

**Technique**: Performance analysis and optimal design of ORB-type GRAND
algorithms. Introduces RS-ORBGRAND (Reshuffled-ORBGRAND) using an average-
guessing-posterior (AGP) framework to determine an offline optimal query
ordering. Claims within 0.1 dB of ML on BCH(127,113).

**Why not bank-worthy**: This appeared in this scan for the first time in
searches (not found in any prior scan's results). However, it is a
theoretical optimization of GRAND query scheduling, not a new decoder
algorithm for LDPC codes. BCH(127,113) has 14 redundancy bits — similar
to FT8's CRC-14 in the hb-267 probe context. The AGP-optimal ordering
could, in principle, improve hb-267's GRAND-on-CRC step's query budget
efficiency. However:
1. hb-267's probe plan already covers Q ∈ {50, 200, 500, 2000} as a
   query-budget sweep — the RS-ORBGRAND ordering is a refinement of
   *which* query pattern to use, not a new architectural approach.
2. The mechanism is an offline precomputed schedule, not an online
   algorithm change — implementable as a lookup table in hb-267's probe.
3. This does not rise to the level of a new hypothesis; it is a potential
   refinement within hb-267's probe plan.
Not bank-worthy as a standalone entry; noted as a potential probe-plan
refinement for hb-267 (try RS-ORBGRAND scheduling in addition to standard
ORBGRAND scheduling as a secondary arm).
