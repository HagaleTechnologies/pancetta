## Ranked candidates

### Rank 1 — LogLab linear drift de-chirp via three-Costas joint power fit (hb-277 draft)

**Technique:** ckarc/LogLab (GitHub; author: Brian Krenzin K9KSU, Central Kansas
Amateur Radio Club W0CY), PR #71 "FT8 decoder: follow drift, validate by differential
coherence, gate deep decodes by evidence" (October 2026, AGPL-3.0).

The PR adds a linear-drift estimator that fits a joint (frequency offset, linear drift
rate) model to the in-phase power of all three Costas arrays. Once the drift rate is
accepted (it must explain ≥10% more Costas power than a steady-frequency fit —
`DRIFT_MARGIN`), the baseband is de-chirped before LLR extraction. The same PR also
adds: (a) a **differential coherence** post-decode validation (multiply each decoded
tone by conjugate of the next symbol's tone, sum; threshold 1.8 — see §Noted below),
and (b) **OSD evidence gating** (deep-search result accepted only when leading the
runner-up by ≥1 and either two likelihood sets agree or signal "evenness" ≥0.4).

**Measured evidence (self-reported by PR author on a bench corpus of 400 signals per
condition unless noted):**

| Condition | PR #71 | Baseline (stage 1) | WSJT-X 3.0.2 |
|-----------|-------:|-------------------:|-------------:|
| Drift 0.5 Hz/s | **307** | 151 | 273 |
| Drift 0.1 Hz/s | 342 | 317 | (not given) |
| Rayleigh 0.5 | 271 | 278 | (not given) |
| Clean seed 1 / 9 / 10 | 485 / 491 / 479 | 485 / 491 / 481 | (not given) |
| Near-miss wrong CRCs | 24 | 60 | (not given) |
| False decodes | **0** | — | — |
| CPU | +3% | — | — |

The 307 vs 273 on 0.5 Hz/s drift is a +12.5% improvement over WSJT-X 3.0.2 (without
AP; WSJT-X +AP would be higher but was not benchmarked for this condition in the PR).
The clean-set performance is essentially unchanged (479–491 vs 481–491), confirming
no do-no-harm regression. 60 → 24 wrong CRCs on clean signals suggests the evidence
gating also reduces OSD false positives, independent of drift.

**Primary source:**
- GitHub PR: https://github.com/ckarc/LogLab/pull/71
- Project: https://github.com/ckarc/LogLab (AGPL-3.0)

**Applicability to pancetta:**
- pancetta's Costas sync (`pancetta-ft8/src/costas_sync.rs`) fits a single frequency
  offset from the three Costas arrays using a 2D power grid. It does not fit a linear
  drift rate. The LogLab approach adds one more parameter (Hz/s slope) to the same
  power-grid search over the three anchor-block locations.
- The de-chirp is applied to the raw complex baseband before DFT extraction. This maps
  cleanly to pancetta's pipeline: de-chirp the complex waveform after costas_sync and
  before `extract_ft8_symbols`. The chirp rate is (drift_hz_per_s × T_symbol) for a
  GFSK symbol of duration 1/6.25 Hz ≈ 0.160 s.
- Target population: signals with transmitter LO drift (common on cheap SDRs), Doppler
  from mobile paths, or accumulated drift over the 12.64 s slot. The 12.64 s slot
  × 1 Hz/s drift = 12.64 Hz shift end-to-end (nearly one tone bin); at 0.5 Hz/s the
  shift is ~6 Hz. This is exactly the regime where pancetta's current static sync fails.
- Relationship to hb-076 (per-block phase rotors for intra-slot drift): hb-076 operates
  *after* LLR extraction (phase correction per Costas block) and is DOWNGRADED/PENDING.
  hb-277 operates *before* LLR extraction (de-chirp the waveform) and is therefore
  architecturally upstream. Both can coexist; hb-277 is likely more effective because
  it removes drift before the DFT bins are contaminated.
- License: AGPL-3.0. The PR description gives full algorithmic detail (margin threshold,
  de-chirp formula, Costas power grid use) without requiring the source to be read.
  A clean-room spec can be written from the PR text alone. Reader writes spec;
  implementer never opens the AGPL source.
- Priority relative to bank: Comparable to hb-271 AP llrc (+0.36 dB, no training) and
  hb-273 (gated OSD-3, +0.35 dB). Drift correction targets a *different* failure mode
  (drifting signals) rather than marginal-SNR clean signals, so it is additive rather
  than competing with hb-271/hb-273. pancetta's hard-200 corpus likely underrepresents
  drifting signals (most captured from a local reference-quality SDR); a targeted drift
  corpus is needed to measure the actual gain.

### Rank 2 — LogLab fading channel classifier + eigenvector symbol combining (hb-278 draft)

**Technique:** ckarc/LogLab PR #73 "FT8 decoder: classify the channel, combine symbols
to suit it, and lock through fading" (October 2026, AGPL-3.0).

The PR adds a five-hypothesis fading channel model estimated from the Costas tones.
The five hypotheses are Gaussian-channel likelihoods fit to the in-phase power of
the sync arrays. The winning hypothesis gives a "phase correlation" estimate (how
quickly the channel phase changes). Data symbols are then combined using the
**eigenvectors of that hypothesis's correlation matrix** — a matched linear combiner
that is the coherent sum for a steady channel, the incoherent power sum when phase is
scrambled, and an interpolated blend in between. Frequency and drift are refit against
the classified channel; the classification–refit loop runs up to three iterations
until settled. For the deep search, the OSD symbol-set selection (which likelihoods
are "steady" enough for 3-or-4-symbol sets) is also conditioned on the channel class.

**Measured evidence (self-reported, 400 signals per condition, seed 1):**

| Condition | PR #73 | PR #71 | WSJT-X 3.0.2 (no AP) |
|-----------|-------:|-------:|---------------------:|
| 2 Hz Rayleigh spread | 238 | 271 | 253 |
| 2 Hz phase noise | 238 | 227 | (not given) |
| Echo + 1 Hz Doppler | 287 | 271 | (not given) |
| Drift 0.1 Hz/s | 339 | 342 | (not given) |
| 32-condition sweep | **8,818** | 8,779 | **8,747** (no AP); WSJT-X+AP ~8,900+ (est.) |
| Held-out seed 9 | 2,402 | 2,374 | (not given) |
| 30 dB deep fade | 165 | 173 | (regression vs #71) |
| False decodes | **0** | 0 | — |
| CPU | +30% over #71, ~3.9 s / 15 s slot | +3% | — |

The 32-condition sweep shows PR #73 at 8,818 vs WSJT-X 3.0.2 (no AP) at 8,747 (+71,
+0.8%). WSJT-X with its AP decodes is likely around 8,900, so the net gap vs the
full WSJT-X pipeline is not measured. The 30% CPU overhead is significant (3.9 s on
4 cores; pancetta's 15-second slot gives roughly the same wall-clock budget).

**Primary source:**
- GitHub PR: https://github.com/ckarc/LogLab/pull/73
- GitHub PR: https://github.com/ckarc/LogLab/pull/71 (predecessor)
- Project: https://github.com/ckarc/LogLab (AGPL-3.0)

**Applicability to pancetta:**
- pancetta's LLR extraction (`pancetta-ft8/src/extract.rs`, `llr.rs`) assumes a
  single-channel steady model. The eigenvector combiner would replace or extend the
  current max-log LLR computation with a per-symbol channel-conditioned weight.
- The five fading hypotheses require estimating channel phase correlation from the
  Costas tones, then selecting the matched combiner. This adds a channel-estimation
  pass between costas_sync and extract — a nontrivial architectural addition.
- The 30% CPU cost is a constraint: pancetta must complete decoding within the 15-second
  FT8 slot. At 3.9 s/slot on 4 cores (LogLab bench) vs pancetta's current ~2–3 s, the
  overhead is marginal but real. `Ft8Config::max_candidates` and the OSD budget would
  need to be re-examined.
- The improvement on a 32-condition fading sweep is real but modest (+71 vs WSJT-X
  without AP). On real HF, fading-dominant slots are a subset; most pancetta operating
  time is on AWGN-like or mildly fading paths.
- Relationship to hb-277: hb-278 is architecturally downstream; it builds on the
  drift estimator (hb-277) and extends it to a full channel classifier. Probe hb-277
  first; hb-278 makes sense only if hb-277 ships and the drift corpus shows residual
  fading failures.
- License: AGPL-3.0 → clean-room spec firewall. Same project as hb-277.

## Noted but not bank-worthy

### LogLab differential coherence codeword validation (PR #71)

**Technique:** After LDPC decode, multiply each decoded tone by the conjugate of the
next symbol's tone; sum the products. This is a tone-to-tone phase-step coherence
score. Threshold 1.8 → zero false passes in 480,000 trials. A "run coherence" test
(threshold 30, covering groups of four symbols) handles frequency-selective paths.

**Why not a separate hypothesis:** This is a post-decode validation filter that
complements pancetta's existing LLR whitening and callsign-trust FP filters. The
marginal FP-reduction benefit on pancetta's pipeline is unclear — pancetta's FP rate
on hard-200 is already below 0.5% per slot. The technique is noted here for
completeness; folding it into the hb-277 probe as a do-no-harm gate is the preferred
path (the OSD evidence gating in PR #71 already incorporates the differential
coherence score as one criterion).

### LogLab OSD evidence gating (PR #71)

Accepts a deep-search OSD result only when it leads the runner-up by ≥1 and either
two likelihood sets agree or signal "evenness" ≥0.4 (Costas in-phase / total power).
Reduced wrong CRCs from 60 to 24 on clean signals. Structurally related to hb-273
(gated OSD-3, +0.35 dB, highest confirmed priority). Note for the hb-273 probe: the
"leading margin + likelihood agreement" criterion is an alternative to whatever gating
mechanism hb-273 uses; evaluate both in the probe.
