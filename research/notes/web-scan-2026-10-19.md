## Ranked candidates

### Rank 1 — fastldpc CRC-linearized OSD-3 (ft2.it / Decodium 4.0 Core Shannon)

**Technique:** A from-scratch decoder for the shared FT8/FT4/FT2 LDPC(174,91) code that
uses the linearity of the CRC-14 to reduce OSD-3 candidate evaluation cost. Each
candidate test vector's syndrome can be computed from precomputed per-bit-flip syndrome
contributions rather than a full matrix-vector product, making individual candidate trials
cheaper without reducing their count. This enables OSD at depth 3 (approximately C(174,3)
≈ 836K candidate trials) at a claimed throughput of ~16× the WSJT-X OSD-2 chain. The
decoder is integrated into Decodium 4.0 "Core Shannon" and tested on LDPC(174,91) in
AWGN at 20,000 codewords per point.

**Primary source:** `ft2.it/fastldpc/` — the ft2.it project's technical article describing
the fastldpc decoder. DNS-blocked in this environment; details obtained from a search
snippet. Full construction procedure (soft-distance gate parameters, candidate-set limits,
FP control mechanisms beyond CRC gating) NOT confirmed — requires direct article read.

**Measured evidence (self-reported, AWGN simulations only):**

| Comparison | Reported gain |
|---|---|
| fastldpc vs. plain min-sum (NMS) | +1.3 dB |
| fastldpc vs. WSJT-X-style OSD-2 | +0.35 dB |
| False-decode rate (AWGN) | Claimed equal to OSD-2 |
| Throughput vs. WSJT-X OSD-2 | ~16× faster |

These are developer-published bench figures on a simulated single-signal AWGN channel.
They have not been independently replicated.

**Relationship to hb-034 (SHELVED):** hb-034 was shelved because exhaustive OSD-3 on
LDPC(174,91) produced +284 FP novels on the `curated-hard-200` real-signal corpus
(multi-signal busy-band WAVs), tracing to CRC-14 collisions at depth-3 search. The
fastldpc article claims "equal false-decode rates" — but this claim is measured on AWGN
single-signal simulations, not on real-signal busy-band scenarios. The CRC-14 linearized
syndrome optimization is a *cost reduction* per candidate trial, not a reduction in the
number of candidates searched or a filter on which candidates are accepted. It does NOT
inherently address CRC-14 collisions: in an AWGN single-signal scenario, CRC-14
collisions at depth 3 are rare because the unreliable bits are truly random; in a
busy-band multi-signal scenario, interfering signal energy is correlated with the target
codeword's reliability ordering, increasing the probability of high-scoring spurious
candidates that happen to satisfy CRC-14.

The "equal false-decode rate" claim from AWGN simulations therefore does NOT resolve
whether the hb-034 failure mode (busy-band FP rate) is addressed. If the fastldpc
construction additionally includes a soft-distance gate (reject candidates whose channel
LLR distance exceeds a threshold) or an early-exit rule based on candidate score, those
mechanisms could limit FP rate in real-signal scenarios — but this cannot be confirmed
without reading the full article. **The prior scan's caution stands: do not pre-apply
hb-034's shelved verdict until the construction is confirmed.**

**Applicability to pancetta:**
- pancetta uses SP-BP (sum-product belief propagation), not NMS. The reported +1.3 dB
  is a composite of SP-BP replacing NMS (~0.8–0.9 dB, already captured in pancetta's
  baseline) plus OSD-3 depth increase (~0.35 dB incremental over OSD-2). The actionable
  number for pancetta is the +0.35 dB OSD-3 marginal gain.
- `pancetta-ft8/src/decoder.rs` already integrates OSD. Current production OSD depth
  (`maxosd=0` for most passes; ndepth gate controls access to higher depths) would need
  to be extended to depth 3.
- The CRC-14 linearized syndrome technique itself is directly applicable to any OSD
  implementation on LDPC(174,91) since CRC-14 is a linear code over the same field.
  Implementation would operate on `decoder.rs`'s CRC computation path.
- **Probe design prerequisite:** Confirm the full construction from the ft2.it article —
  specifically whether a soft-distance gate or candidate-count limit bounds the FP rate.
  If yes, open hb-273 and design the probe to test OSD-3 with that gate on both hard-200
  recall and noise_1000 FP gate. If no additional FP control exists beyond CRC gating,
  this reduces to the hb-034 failure mode: +0.35 dB recall at the cost of unacceptable
  FP rate on busy bands. Do NOT implement before confirming the construction.
- Hard-200 corpus probe plan (conditional on construction confirm): test `maxosd=3` with
  the CRC-14 linearized syndrome speedup; gate against noise_1000 zero-tolerance;
  use a separate design corpus for soft-distance threshold selection (not hard-200 itself)
  to avoid overfitting bias.

**License:** GPL-3.0 (confirmed in search snippet: "integrated into Decodium 4.0 'Core
Shannon' and released under GPL-3.0"). Clean-room spec firewall applies. The technical
article at ft2.it/fastldpc/ is separately authored explanatory text — safe to read for
the concept and construction procedure (spec-firewall blocks reading the source code
itself, not the article).

### Rank 2 — Neural-Model-Augmented Hybrid NMS-OSD (arXiv:2509.25580)

**Technique:** A serial hybrid decoder that places a lightweight convolutional neural
network between a normalized min-sum (NMS) decoder and an ordered statistics decoder
(OSD). After NMS BP runs, the CNN refines the per-bit reliability ordering before OSD
uses it to select the most-reliable positions. The CNN is trained with a loss function
that enforces non-negativity and order consistency of reliability estimates. An adaptive
decoding path skips OSD when NMS succeeds (analogous to the standard BP→OSD gate), and a
sliding-window early-exit rule terminates OSD search before exhausting candidates. A
separate "false-convergence detector" catches NMS outputs that pass parity checks but are
likely wrong, sending them to OSD.

**Primary source:** arXiv:2509.25580, "Neural-Model-Augmented Hybrid NMS-OSD Decoders
for Near-ML in Short Block Codes," Guangwen Li and Xiao Yu. arXiv submission September
29, 2025; published as a journal article in EURASIP Journal on Wireless Communications
and Networking (Springer) in 2026. arxiv.org DNS-blocked; abstract obtained via
Semantic Scholar ADS entry and Moonlight literature review.

**Measured evidence:**

- Near-ML FER results on LDPC(128,64), BCH (code family), and RS codes (code families)
- Gains in throughput, latency, and complexity vs plain BP+OSD reported (specific dB
  numbers not available from search snippet; full PDF DNS-blocked)
- False-convergence detector catches NMS errors that would otherwise pass parity

**Applicability to pancetta:**
- pancetta uses SP-BP (sum-product), not NMS. The CNN here is trained specifically on
  NMS output distributions (NMS has a characteristic min-approximation error pattern
  that the CNN corrects). Applying this to SP-BP would require retraining the CNN on
  SP-BP output distributions; the NMS-trained model would not transfer directly.
- Training requires a labeled corpus derived from LDPC(174,91) decoding failure modes.
  The `pancetta-research` harness could generate this, but it is non-trivial work.
- The false-convergence detector (catching parity-passing wrong decodes) is architecturally
  interesting for any BP variant — pancetta's OSD bypass condition (`crc_ok_after_bp`)
  could be made more selective using a similar reliability-based gate.
- The early-exit OSD rule is a simpler version of hb-272 (DET, arXiv:2609.26215) applied
  to the OSD stage specifically (not the ensemble outer loop).
- The code families tested (LDPC 128,64; BCH; RS) do not include LDPC(174,91). Transfer
  to the FT8 code is an assumption.

**License:** arXiv preprint (open access); Springer EURASIP journal (open access CC-BY).
No GPL source involved. No spec-firewall requirement.

### SECS (hb-270, arXiv:2608.09563) — code families confirmed: NOT LDPC

The SECS paper abstract details are now confirmed via search: tests on **BCH (63,30)**,
**PG(2,8) code** (overcomplete projective-plane-based code), and **cyclic (105,53)**. The
paper does NOT include results on any LDPC code family, and does not include LDPC(174,91)
specifically. This weakens the applicability assessment compared to RBE (hb-269), which
at least tested codes as short as MET(100,50) and CCSDS(128,64).

The SECS mechanism (merging soft outputs from shallow BP members, then OSD re-encodes
from the merged reliability ordering) is structurally applicable to any linear block code,
but the performance numbers are for non-LDPC cyclic/BCH codes. For FT8's LDPC(174,91),
the ensemble diversity premise must be validated on the actual code graph before the
claimed "gap-closing" result can be expected to transfer. No new change to hb-270 status
(PENDING, probe on LDPC(174,91) needed); updated applicability note only.

**Revised applicability note for hb-270:** The paper's "gap-closing" claims are on
non-LDPC cyclic codes; the mechanism is code-agnostic but the magnitude of gain on
LDPC(174,91) (an irregular, high-girth code optimized for BP) is entirely uncharacterized.
Ensemble diversity may be lower on LDPC(174,91) than on the BCH/cyclic codes tested. The
probe must establish whether diversity exists before expecting the SECS LLR-combining
stage to provide a meaningful reliability reordering.

### ft2.it FT8 Decoder Shootout — new four-decoder benchmark context

The ft2.it website published an on-air four-decoder comparison test (page:
`ft2.it/ft8-shootout/`; DNS-blocked but confirmed via search) comparing Decodium 1.0.611,
JTDX 2.2.159, **WSJT-X 3.2.0** (labeled without "rc1" — may be GA release; see note),
and MSHV 2.76.5 on a shared 7.074 MHz audio feed for approximately 70 minutes.

**Reported decode counts across 4 rounds:**

| Decoder | Reported total (unique) |
|---|---|
| Decodium 1.0.611 | 850 |
| JTDX 2.2.159 | 668 |
| WSJT-X 3.2.0 | 650 |
| MSHV 2.76.5 | 78 |

Decodium lead over WSJT-X: ~31% (round 1 default settings) to ~66% (round 4 deep).

**Critical methodology note:** This test was run by the Decodium / ft2.it developers.
Decodium's settings were kept at default; JTDX, WSJT-X, and MSHV were raised to their
deepest search settings in rounds 2–4. The relative improvement from deep settings for
WSJT-X is therefore partially absorbed by rounds 2–4, but Decodium was already at full
depth. The test is a single 70-minute session on one antenna and one band; no independent
replication has been found. Treat as a vendor benchmark with plausibility, not an
audited result.

**WSJT-X 3.2.0 GA status:** The shootout page labels the WSJT-X version as "3.2.0"
(not "rc1"). An independent search found WSJT-X 3.0.0 GA released 2026-04-06; a user
guide at `wsjtx.github.io/wsjtx/wsjtx-main-3.2_en.html` exists, suggesting a 3.2 guide
has been published. Whether 3.2.0 GA shipped before this shootout is unconfirmed — the
shoot-out may have used rc1 and labeled it "3.2.0". Check the WSJT-X download page or
release announcements for confirmation.

**Relevance to existing hypotheses:** This benchmark is context for hb-271 (WSJT-X
3.2.0-rc1 decoder techniques). The large Decodium lead is driven by its fastldpc OSD-3
chain (now tracked as draft hb-273) plus AP/subtraction mechanisms already tracked in
the bank. The MSHV decode count of 78 (vs 650+ for the others) is consistent with MSHV's
MAM-focused (multi-answer protocol) design, which prioritizes running-QSO throughput
over weak-signal sensitivity.

## Noted but not bank-worthy

### arXiv:2609.03256 — Iterative Semantic Decoding (NOT applicable)

"Iterative Semantic Decoding for Short Block Codes" (Hao, Yue, Nie, Vucetic, Li;
U. Sydney; Sep 3, 2026). 5 pages, cs.IT. Pairs OSD with a natural-language language-model
denoiser in a feedback loop for semantic communications (SNLI corpus NLP). Transmitter
uses a character-level interleaver + short block code; receiver alternates OSD ↔ LM
denoising with character-level verification feedback. Reported +1.5 dB BLER gain on SNLI
over AWGN. NOT applicable to FT8: the LM prior targets natural-language byte streams, and
FT8's structured message vocabulary (callsigns, grid squares, signal reports) differs
fundamentally from SNLI text. AP (hb-048/hb-271) already injects structured FT8-specific
priors. An FT8-trained source model would be a new separate line of work — no such model
has been proposed.

### arXiv:2607.14589 — Quasi-BP + Neural Check Node for BCH (NOT applicable)

"Quasi-Belief Propagation and Neural-Network Check Node Processing for BCH Codes"
(Guangwen Li; Jul 16, 2026; 6 pages, cs.IT). Replaces tanh/arctanh check-node operations
with a CNN for BCH codes, using code automorphisms and redundant parity-check matrices for
graph diversity. Within 0.25 dB of LDPC-equivalent-blocklength FER. NOT applicable to
FT8: targets BCH codes specifically (automorphism-based redundant H construction requires
a BCH structure); LDPC(174,91) does not have the same exploitable automorphism group.
Different from hb-269/hb-270/hb-272 (those use permutation- or row-boost diversity on H,
not automorphism-based redundant H). Not bank-worthy.
